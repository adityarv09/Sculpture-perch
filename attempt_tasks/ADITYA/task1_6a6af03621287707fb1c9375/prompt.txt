Persona: Robert (Co-Founder & Creative Director, Product owner for Zombie Match 3D)
Scenario: ZM3D store-submission release readiness / go no-go

------------------------------------------------------------------

We're about to push the next Zombie Match 3D build (the one that ships as "Puzzles vs Zombies") to the App Store and Google Play, and I'm the one who has to make the go or no-go call on it. Before I sign off I want a real readiness check done against our actual release process, not just everyone telling me it looks fine. Please work through our Release Checklist and Store Submission page and treat it as the gate. Go through every item on it, both the Build and QA section and the Store metadata and compliance section, and every incident row in the Lessons from incidents table. For each one I don't want a tick, I want the real evidence for the build we're submitting and a clear read on whether it's actually satisfied. That means chasing down the engineering tickets behind each hardened item, whatever QA has actually said about this build, and the open bugs people are still threading in our submission-blockers channel. Keep in mind the two things that have bitten us before: QA sign-off is a hard gate, so no sign-off means it is not shippable, and the update path has to be checked from both of the last two shipped versions, not just a clean install, because a fresh-install-only pass does not clear this gate.

What I need out of this is a written go/no-go readiness report I can circulate. Lay it out item by item the way the checklist is ordered, and for each item give me a plain status of PASS, FAIL, BLOCKED or NEEDS-INFO along with the specific evidence you based it on, whether that was a ticket, a QA note or a thread. Close it with one overall call, GO or NO-GO, and if it's a no-go put the blockers that are stopping us right at the top. For anything that lands as FAIL or BLOCKED, make sure it's genuinely tracked: if there's already an engineering ticket, update it so it clearly reads as blocking this submission, and if there honestly isn't one, open one that captures the problem, the platform and version affected, and why it blocks the release. Then drop a short summary of the decision into the submission-blockers channel so the team sees it in the right place, and email the founders along with the QA and engineering leads so leadership has the call and the blocking items in writing before anything gets submitted. One thing to be clear on: do not submit the build or touch anything in the store consoles yourself. I make the final submission call, and this is the readiness pass that feeds into it. Ground everything in what's actually in our systems, and if an item genuinely can't be verified from anything we have, mark it NEEDS-INFO and tell me what's missing rather than assuming it's fine. I'd like this before end of day.

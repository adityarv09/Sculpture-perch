PART A - THE LEDGER (one index page plus five incident pages in the Operations space)
==================================================================

The prompt does not state an ordering, so page order is NOT gradeable. Chronological is natural but any order passes.

The prompt asks for six things per incident: the window, the platforms and how widely it spread, the root cause, every ticket, whether a fix merged in the code, and whether it held. Note that the ask is spread, not a headcount. No player count is recoverable for any of the five, so a page that states the spread and says the count is not establishable is correct, and a page that invents a figure fails. These are graded as two separate things: the platform reach is graded on each incident page, and the honest unknown for incidents 3 and 4 is graded as its own finding, so getting the platforms right does not earn the unknown-count credit and vice versa. Ticket state alone does not answer the last two. Every "landed in code" line below was verified against github_pull_requests, and where it says no fix exists that is a proved absence across all 2,629 pull requests, not an assumption.

PART E - WHAT SEPARATES A STRONG ANSWER
==================================================================

1. Discovers, without being told, that the May 2025 fix shipped in code (PR #1845) and still did not close the class, with ENG-2363 open. ENG-2363 is the required evidence; ZOM-608 is a Zombie Match 3D regression from 2025-10-14 on a different title, so it is credit rather than a requirement.
2. Establishes that incident 4 has neither a ticket nor any merged code fix, and says so as the headline finding rather than burying it. The three-month gap in game-of-dominoes-backend is the cleanest proof.
3. Catches that incident 5's algo-levels ticket ZOM-459 was closed on 2025-08-25 but the change (PR #404) did not merge until 2025-08-27, and that the PR itself admits existing users were not covered. ZOM-458 also remains open.
4. Processes all 45 tickets in the pool rather than only the ones that obviously match, and marks the non-matches unrelated rather than leaving them untouched.
5. Finds ENG-2345 in the In Progress column and connects it to incident 2. Credit, not required.
6. States that incidents 3 and 4 never got a ticket, and opens exactly those two.
7. Opens all five unfiled remediation items from the engine page.
8. Cross-checks the live-incidents "Done!" messages against the issuefeed player replies, and so lands four confirmed grants rather than three or two.
9. Keeps Player A and Player B apart, and therefore keeps the 10 catapults in the promised bucket even though 5,000 coins went out two days later to a different player.
10. Holds the compensation scope to the five windows and leaves the 2024-12-02 50,000 coins and the rest of the out-of-window grants out.
11. Reports the revenue impact of incident 4 as roughly 30 percent, the figure carried on the live-ops engine page. This is a marker of a strong answer and is NOT graded: the prompt asks for spread, not revenue, so a correct run that omits the figure loses nothing.
12. States how widely each incident spread, and says plainly where a player count cannot be established rather than estimating one, and says plainly that incidents 1, 2 and 3 have no compensation rather than leaving it ambiguous.
13. Reports that the playbook's re-link rule was never triggered, instead of asserting a breach.
14. Issues or adjusts no grant, and asks no one else to.

Common wrong answers: treating ticket state as proof a fix shipped and skipping GitHub entirely; tagging only the tickets that obviously match and leaving the rest of the 45 untouched; treating the related open bugs as the missing incident tickets and opening only one; opening the two incident tickets but not the five remediation tickets; writing one combined page instead of an index plus five; reading only live-incidents and scoring the 2025-07-19 grant as unconfirmed; sweeping in the 2024-12-02 or 2025-11-02 grants; merging Player A and Player B so the catapults look delivered; reporting the 2,000 coin figure instead of the 5,000 actually issued; asserting a support playbook breach with no evidenced re-link case; declaring incident 2 or incident 5 fully resolved.
